Language for Advice

Giving advice in English

When you write your infographic, you will need to give clear advice. There are many ways to do this in English. Here are the most useful structures at B1.2 level:

1. Modal verbs

  • should / shouldn't + infinitive (without "to") — the most common way to give advice.
    You should turn off your notifications at night. / You shouldn't check your phone at the dinner table.
  • ought to + infinitive — slightly more formal than "should".
    You ought to take a digital break every weekend.
  • had better + infinitive — stronger advice, with a sense of warning.
    You'd better stop scrolling if you want to sleep tonight.

2. Suggestions and recommendations

  • Why don't you + infinitive? Why don't you leave your phone in another room?
  • How about / What about + -ing? How about deleting the app for a week?
  • Try + -ing. Try setting a daily time limit on Instagram.

3. Imperatives — short and powerful

Imperatives are very common in infographics: Turn off notifications. / Don't check your phone before sleeping. / Set a screen-time limit.

4. Useful expressions

  • It's a good idea to + infinitive.
  • It helps to + infinitive.
  • One way to … is to + infinitive.

⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

  • ✗ You should to turn off notifications. ✓ You should turn off notifications.
  • ✗ Why don't you to delete the app? ✓ Why don't you delete the app?
  • ✗ How about to leave the phone outside? ✓ How about leaving the phone outside?
