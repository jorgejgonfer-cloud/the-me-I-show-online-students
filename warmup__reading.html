Warm-up & Reading

Warm-up Questions

Before we begin, take a moment to think honestly about these questions. There are no right or wrong answers — just be honest with yourself.

  1. What is the first thing you do in the morning? And the last thing at night?
  2. How many hours a day do you think you spend on your phone? Check your screen-time settings now and compare. Were you right?
  3. Which app or website do you spend most time on? Why?
  4. Have you ever felt anxious or annoyed after scrolling for a long time? When?
  5. Has your phone ever stopped you from doing something you really wanted to do (sleep, read, exercise, talk to someone…)?

Share two of your answers with the person sitting next to you. Listen to their answers carefully — you will need to remember them later.

Reading: Are You Doomscrolling?

Duración:
30 min
Agrupamiento:
Individual + parejas

Read the article and try to understand the three habits it describes. Underline any words you do not know — we will work on them after reading.

Are You Doomscrolling? Three Digital Habits That Are Hurting You

Your phone is probably the first thing you touch in the morning and the last thing you see at night. That alone is not a problem. The problem is what you do during those minutes — and during all the other minutes in the day when you pick up your phone "just for a second" and end up scrolling for half an hour.

Researchers have given names to three of the most common digital habits that, in small doses, are harmless, but that can seriously affect your well-being if they get out of control.

1. Doomscrolling

Doomscrolling is the habit of spending a long time scrolling through bad news. You start by checking the headlines and, twenty minutes later, you have read about a war, a fire, an accident and a celebrity break-up. You feel worse, but you can't stop. Why? Because your brain is looking for safety: "if I keep reading, I will understand the world and feel in control." Unfortunately, the opposite happens — you feel anxious and powerless.

2. FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)

FOMO is the uncomfortable feeling that everyone else is having a better time than you. You see your friends at a concert, on a beach, at a wedding — and you are at home folding laundry. The truth, of course, is that they are also at home most of the time, but they only post the highlights. FOMO can make you say yes to plans you don't really want, and feel guilty when you say no.

3. Social comparison

Social media platforms are designed to show you the best version of other people: their nicest holidays, their funniest jokes, their most attractive photos. After half an hour of scrolling, it is easy to feel that your own life is boring or that you are not good enough. This is called upward social comparison, and studies have shown that it is linked to lower self-esteem, especially in young adults.

What can you do?

The good news is that you don't need to throw your phone away. Small changes — turning off notifications, leaving the phone in another room at night, unfollowing accounts that make you feel bad — can have a big effect. The first step is simply noticing what you are doing. That is what this unit is about.

EVALUATION (group discussion)

After reading, discuss in groups of 3:

  • Which of the three habits do YOU recognise in yourself the most?
  • Why do you think it is so hard to stop, even when we feel bad?
  • What was the most surprising thing you read?
  • If you had to give one piece of advice to a younger sibling about social media, what would it be?

d) They are bored.
